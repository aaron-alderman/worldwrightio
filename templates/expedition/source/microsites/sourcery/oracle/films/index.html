The Moving Picture Book

The same fable, played out on a toy stage. Every cut-out, curtain and splash is drawn in code, composited frame by frame, and signed with a receipt, like the spells in the story.

The flood, forever

Eight seconds that loop without a seam: every bob and wave completes a whole number of cycles, so the last frame leads straight back to the first.

How it was made

  1. Cut-outs. Every figure, backdrop, curtain and title card is drawn as SVG on one sprite sheet and rasterised by headless Chrome to transparent PNGs, set in the site’s own fonts. Each cut-out gets a cream paper margin and a cast shadow, like a toy theatre piece.
  2. The stage. ffmpeg composites the cut-outs with overlay time expressions: brooms hop and bob on sine curves, the water rises on a smoothstep, backdrops drift for parallax, and each new broom splits off an old one and slides to its place.
  3. The paint. Watercolour washes are computed pixel by pixel with geq: wobbly ellipses with pigment pooling at the edges and a little granulation. A paper grain is multiplied over every frame.
  4. The theatre. A painted proscenium frames the whole film, velvet curtains open and close on the title and the moral, and the scenes change with xfade page wipes and an iris.
  5. The sound. A music box played by aevalsrc, a pentatonic waltz of decaying bell tones, with page-turn swishes shaped from filtered pink noise.

The film’s witness

The pipeline hashes its own sources and every file it renders. The site’s tests recompute both, so a film that no longer matches its sources can’t slip through unnoticed.
