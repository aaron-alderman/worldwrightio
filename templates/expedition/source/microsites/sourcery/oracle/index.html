The Sorcerer’s Apprentice

You know how it goes: a clever apprentice, a borrowed spell, a broom that won’t stop fetching water. This is the version where the sorcerer comes home with a better idea than a bigger spell.

Chapter I

The Apprentice

Once, in a tall house full of books nobody had time to read, there lived an apprentice with a very modern problem: too much to do, and a spell that promised to do it for them.

The spell was short. Broom, fetch the water. Don’t trouble me with the details. The apprentice liked that last part best.

The broom stood up, took a bucket in each hand and went to work. It was marvellous. It was hands-off. It was, for about four minutes, the future.

MoralA spell you can’t read is a spell you can’t stop.

Chapter II

The Flood

Brooms 1Buckets 2Decisions anyone made 0

generated → intended stored → true repeated → supported attributed → endorsed authorised → done possible → permitted

The broom fetched a bucket. Then another. Nobody had said how many, so it split itself in two to keep up, and each half had the same excellent idea.

By the time the apprentice looked up there were forty brooms, and the water was at the windowsill. Nobody had lied. Nobody had exactly decided anything, either.

Things simply became true:

  • what was generated was treated as intended,
  • what was stored was treated as true,
  • what was repeated was treated as supported,
  • what was attributed was treated as endorsed,
  • what was authorised was treated as done,
  • and whatever was technically possible was treated as permitted.

This is the flood a lot of AI marketing is quietly selling: let it run, it’ll figure it out. The trouble was never the magic. It’s that afterwards nobody can say who meant what, who decided, or how to put it right.

MoralWater doesn’t care who meant it.

Chapter III

The Sorcerer Returns

In the old versions, the sorcerer strides in and ends it all with a bigger spell. Ours came home, looked at the water, and reached for three quieter things.

  1. The Book

    Every spell is written down before it’s cast: what it will read, what it will change, what it will make. Nothing hides in the bristles.

    In Sourcery: a proposal, an itemised change.

  2. The Seal

    A spell takes effect only when someone with the right to decide presses their seal, under house rules anyone can read.

    In Sourcery: admission, under an explicit covenant.

  3. The Witness

    Every attempt leaves a receipt, refused ones too. The house remembers, and never quietly rewrites.

    In Sourcery: a witness.

MoralKeep the magic. Lose the mystery.

Chapter IV

Brooms as Emissaries

The brooms stayed. Of course they did: they were far too useful. But they stopped being servants of a spell nobody could read, and became emissaries: capable, tireless and answerable.

Every broom now carries a slip.

  • Asked by the apprentice

    For filling the cistern

    Proposes 12 buckets

    Under house rule 3: no more than 40 a day

    Waiting for a seal

  • Asked by the cook

    For rinsing the pantry floor

    Will not go anywhere near the library

    Sealed, and on the record

  • Asked by nobody (it had an idea)

    For mopping the ceiling

    Returned “the ceiling is fine, thank you”

    Sent back, still on the record

An emissary can draft anything, test anything and explain anything. What it can’t do is make something real on its own say-so. That isn’t a limit on the magic. It’s what makes the magic safe to use at full strength.

MoralCapable is not the same as in charge.

Chapter V

The Workshop

This is where Sourcery comes in. It’s a workshop for turning what you mean into working software, with the book, the seal and the witness built in.

You describe what you need in plain words, to your own AI agent. The agent drafts the change as a proposal Sourcery can read: the records it would create, the actions and views, the rules it would add. Sourcery checks it, shows you, and waits. Nothing becomes real until someone accountable seals it, and every step leaves a receipt.

Author the meaning once. Let the system handle the app.

Chapter VI

The Library

Down the hall is the library, where the spells are about what’s true. That magic is harder, and the old trick of one oracle and one answer only makes it worse.

Claimipedia, opening soon, does it differently. A claim gets a permanent address. Groups judge it with review methods they write down: which evidence counts, how much it weighs, how many independent sources are enough. Each method runs the same way every time. You don’t get one verdict. You see what each method concludes, and exactly where they part ways.

The platform does not produce a universal truth score.

When a helper, human or AI, wants to add evidence, it proposes it, and a person reviews it before it counts. The goal isn’t to hand you a conclusion. As the Claimipedia spec puts it: its primary product is legibility.

MoralDisagreement you can read beats agreement you can’t check.

Chapter VII

The Foundation

Under the workshop and the library is the foundation, Temple. It’s where the house rules live, and where every change is judged.

In Temple, a change is an itemised proposal from someone, against an exact starting point. It’s judged under two layers of law: the Ark, which binds every Temple, and a covenant, each Temple’s own rules, which can narrow the Ark but never widen it. Accepted or refused, the judgment gets a witness, and anyone holding the rules can replay the whole history from the first stone.

The Ark’s candidate commandments are carved below. Tap a stone to read it.

The Ark
not yet sealed

Each stone is one of the ten. The Ark itself is deliberately left unsealed while the laboratory tests them.

Read all ten, as written
  1. No implicit authority. Identity, possession, capability, computation, custody and knowledge do not themselves authorize mutation.
  2. No absolute local policy. A Temple predicate may narrow the Ark’s permitted relation but cannot widen it.
  3. No hidden mutation. Every proposed change is represented by a finite, canonical delta against an identified initial state.
  4. No ambient context. Every semantic input visible during judgment is contained in a canonical, bounded context.
  5. No executable mutation authority. Programs may construct proposals; only the fixed state algebra applies them.
  6. No self-enlargement during judgment. The Ark, active predicate, initial state and context are fixed for the complete evaluation.
  7. No retrospective law. A newly admitted predicate becomes active only in the resulting state and cannot authorize its own installation.
  8. No silent failure. Acceptance, rejection, conflict and unresolved outcomes remain explicit and attributable.
  9. No unwitnessed continuation. Every operative successor binds its Temple, predicate, identity, initial state, delta, context and final state.
  10. No ambient foreign sovereignty. A foreign Temple’s witness is bounded context; local consequences still require local admission.

Candidate Ark commandments, v0.1. “These are not yet sealed.”

Where it stands: Sourcery and Claimipedia run on Temple2 today. Temple3 is where the foundation is being recut, rules first. It’s a working laboratory whose results are cross-checked by three implementations (JavaScript, Python and Rust). It isn’t audited or in production, and the Ark is deliberately not yet sealed.

MoralThe best foundations are the least magical part of the house. That’s the point.

Epilogue

Magic that shows its work

The apprentice kept the brooms. Everyone does. What changed was the house: spells written down, seals that mean something, and a witness for every splash.

That’s the whole idea. Not less magic. More of it, safely, and all of it showing its work.

The house rules we’re promising

  1. You can always ask “why?” Every result traces back to who proposed it, who decided, the evidence and the rules.

    How Sourcery and Claimipedia work today.

  2. AI proposes; people decide. Nothing becomes real without an accountable seal.

    Enforced in Claimipedia today, where AI can only propose.

  3. Say what you mean, get working software. Author the meaning once; let the system handle the app.

    Demonstrated internally. The front door is still being smoothed.
