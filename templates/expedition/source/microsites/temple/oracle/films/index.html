The film

The Covenant, in forty seconds.

A trailer rendered entirely from code: a sky computed pixel by pixel, lights that leave trails, the commandments set in type, and a drone synthesised from sine waves and noise. No cameras, no stock footage, no generated video — every frame can be rendered again from the site’s own source.

How it was made

Procedural cinematography, in filtergraphs.

The film is a separate pipeline beside the site. A choreography file sets every scene, plate, light, spark and chime to the tenth of a second; one Python script turns it into picture and sound with ffmpeg and headless Chrome.

The sky
Computed per pixel with geq: an indigo zenith, lamplight low on the horizon, and far stars from a hash of each pixel’s coordinates.
Stars that twinkle
A seamless three-second loop in which every star keeps its own phase, rendered once and looped under every scene.
Embers
Sparse soft motes rising on the scroll filter.
Lights that leave trails
Sprites steered by overlay time expressions, drawn at twice the frame rate, then lagfun and tmix for trails and motion blur.
Bloom and push-ins
split + gblur + blend=screen for the glow; perspective with per-frame expressions for slow, sub-pixel push-ins.
Transitions
xfade dissolves and fades to black between scenes, and a vignette over all.
Type
Plates set by headless Chrome in the site’s own fonts — the judgment, the tablets, the commandments — then faded and composited frame by frame.
The score
An aevalsrc drone on A and E, wind from filtered anoisesrc, and chimes with exponential decay at each turn. Muted until you ask for it.

Its witness

The film has a witness too.

Every file below carries the hash of its bytes, and the whole set carries the hash of the sources that produced it. The site’s tests recompute both: change a single frame’s recipe without re-rendering, and the build refuses to call the film current.

Transcript

What the film says.

  1. The distance. Two lights — a sentence and an action — rush together. “The distance between a sentence and a consequential action is shrinking.”
  2. The quiet substitutions. “In ordinary software, meaning slides.” generated becomes treated as is not intended · stored becomes treated as is not true · authorized becomes treated as is not executed.
  3. One sentence of law. The kernel judgment, C ⊢ (Ark ∧ P, i) S₀ —d / W→ S₁. “Every change in a Temple must satisfy it.”
  4. Ten candidate commandments. The two tablets; then “No implicit authority.”, “No hidden mutation.”, “No silent failure.” — and a broken seal: “Candidate Ark commandments · v0.1 · Not yet sealed.”
  5. Many Temples. A constellation of Temples, witnesses travelling between them. “Many Temples. No emperor.” “A foreign witness is bounded evidence — never a command.”
  6. Law you can execute. The temple, its doorway lit. “Law you can execute. History no one can quietly rewrite.” “The Ark is not yet sealed.”

Temple3 is a v0.1 laboratory and its Ark is unsealed; the film is a speculative trailer, not a product demonstration.
