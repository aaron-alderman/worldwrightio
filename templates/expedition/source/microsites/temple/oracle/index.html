I · The distance

Artificial intelligence makes ideas easier to express, combine, and execute.

That is its promise, but also its danger.

A suggestion can become a policy, a database migration, a financial decision, a public narrative, or a deployed system before the people affected by it understand what happened or who authorized it.

II · The quiet substitutions

In ordinary software, meaning slides.

Authority hides in permissions, defaults and deployment scripts — or in the simple fact that someone happened to act first. And so, without anyone deciding it:

  • generated becomes treated asis not intended
  • stored becomes treated asis not true
  • repeated becomes treated asis not supported
  • attributed becomes treated asis not endorsed
  • reviewed becomes treated asis not believed
  • authorized becomes treated asis not executed
  • causally involved becomes treated asis not responsible
  • technically possible becomes treated asis not permissible

These are not merely data-quality mistakes. They are changes in the political and moral meaning of a system.

How can representations become procedures without silently changing their epistemic, political, or moral type?

III · The judgment

One sentence of law.

Temple answers with a single judgment. Every change in a Temple must satisfy it — no exceptions, no side doors.

CArkP,iS0d/WS1

  1. Read aloud

    Given context C, under the Ark and this Temple’s predicate P, identity i’s delta d takes state S₀ to S₁ — and witness W records the judgment.

  2. C — the evidence on the table

    Everything the judgment may consider sits inside one canonical, bounded context: at most 65,536 bytes. Nothing ambient.

    Commandment IV · No ambient context

  3. ⊢ — is judged valid

    A total, deterministic decision. The same inputs always yield the same judgment, on any conforming machine.

  4. Ark ∧ P — universal law, and local law

    Effective validity is Ark validity and covenant validity. A Temple may narrow the Ark. It can never widen it.

    Commandment II · No absolute local policy

  5. i — who is asking

    An identity bound to a key. “Key possession is necessary for authentication, never sufficient for covenant authorization.”

    Commandment I · No implicit authority

  6. S₀ — the exact starting point

    Named by its state root: one fingerprint of everything. Propose against a stale basis and you are refused.

  7. d — an itemized change order

    What it reads, what it consumes, what it creates. S₁ = (S₀ − consume) ∪ create. Programs may construct proposals; only the fixed algebra applies them.

    Commandment III · No hidden mutation

  8. W — the witness

    A deterministic receipt binding Temple, predicate, identity, initial state, delta, context and final state. Refusals receive one too — and never advance the state.

    Commandment VIII · No silent failure

  9. S₁ — the result

    Its root must equal the root the proposal claimed. Anyone holding the rules can recompute it, from genesis onward.

    Commandment IX · No unwitnessed continuation

  10. Accepted exactly when

    1. all inputs are canonical and bounded;
    2. the proposal’s basis equals the current state root;
    3. the fixed algebra applies the delta;
    4. the claimed final root equals the computed root;
    5. the active covenant returns true;
    6. every Ark invariant holds.

IV · The Ark and the covenant

Universal law. Local law. Only ever narrower.

The Ark defines the universal transition boundary. A Temple’s covenant defines its local permitted relation.

effective validity = Ark validity and covenant validity

So a covenant can be as particular as its community needs — a laboratory, a guild, a council, a family business — and still never grant what the Ark forbids.

V · The commandments

Ten candidate commandments.

Candidate Ark commandments · v0.1 · not yet sealed

  1. I

    No implicit authority.

    Identity, possession, capability, computation, custody and knowledge do not themselves authorize mutation.

  2. II

    No absolute local policy.

    A Temple predicate may narrow the Ark’s permitted relation but cannot widen it.

  3. III

    No hidden mutation.

    Every proposed change is represented by a finite, canonical delta against an identified initial state.

  4. IV

    No ambient context.

    Every semantic input visible during judgment is contained in a canonical, bounded context.

  5. V

    No executable mutation authority.

    Programs may construct proposals; only the fixed state algebra applies them.

  1. VI

    No self-enlargement during judgment.

    The Ark, active predicate, initial state and context are fixed for the complete evaluation.

  2. VII

    No retrospective law.

    A newly admitted predicate becomes active only in the resulting state and cannot authorize its own installation.

  3. VIII

    No silent failure.

    Acceptance, rejection, conflict and unresolved outcomes remain explicit and attributable.

  4. IX

    No unwitnessed continuation.

    Every operative successor binds its Temple, predicate, identity, initial state, delta, context and final state.

  5. X

    No ambient foreign sovereignty.

    A foreign Temple’s witness is bounded context; local consequences still require local admission.

These commandments govern the form of valid Temples. Membership, authority, consent, exit, custody, reconciliation and succession policies remain per-Temple covenant choices expressed inside this space.

“No gate authorizes sealing the Ark. Sealing requires independent implementations, adversarial review and a separate explicit decision.”

            VII · The witness
            
            
            Every judgment leaves a witness.
            
            
            Acceptance, rejection, conflict and unresolved outcomes remain explicit and attributable. Anyone holding the rules can replay a Temple from its genesis and re-judge every step.
            
            
            “A machine owner may destroy or fork local bytes, but cannot produce a continuation under the original identity without a valid witnessed transition.”
            
          
            
          
        
            
      
            

      
      
            
        
            VIII · Many Temples
            
        
            Many Temples. No emperor.
            
        
            
          
          
            
            
            Temples relate only through explicit covenants. A foreign witness is bounded evidence — “never a command”. Local consequences still require local admission.
            
            
              
              
            • “Trust and authority are non-transitive by default.”
              • 
              
            • “Physical nesting, hosting, storage or discovery does not create authority.”
              • 
              
            • “Dissolution is not automatic: no timeout destroys state or grants emergency authority.”
              • 
            
            
            
            In the laboratory: two Temples, west and east, joined by a blocking prepare-and-commit protocol — through a restart, a partial commit, and recovery.
            
          
            
        
            
      
            

      
      
            
        
            IX · The promise
            
        
            What this ground makes possible.
            
        
            The success case is not merely preventing unauthorized AI action. It is creating systems capable of:
            
        
              
          
            • Distributed authorship without dissolved responsibility.
              • 
          
            • Automation without fabricated human review or endorsement.
              • 
          
            • Privacy without unaccountable anonymity.
              • 
          
            • Plural inquiry without epistemic chaos.
              • 
          
            • Shared protocols without universal centralized judgment.
              • 
          
            • Disagreement without historical erasure.
              • 
          
            • Correction without silent rewriting.
              • 
          
            • Powerful execution without hidden sovereignty.
              • 
          
            • Human judgment informed by computation without being replaced by it.
              • 
        
            
        
            The project is not fundamentally about making AI more powerful. It is about ensuring that increasing power does not require decreasing human intelligibility, plurality, responsibility, or capacity for correction.
            
      
            

      
      
            
        
            X · What stands on it
            
        
            The workshop and the library.
            
        
            Temple makes explicit meaning executable. Sourcery makes the path from human intention to executable consequence governable. Claimipedia makes the epistemic, political, and moral distinctions within that path inspectable.
            
        
            
          
            
            
            Sourcery · the workshop
            
            
            Say what you need. Keep the receipt.
            
            
            You describe the work in conversation. People and AI emissaries draft, test and propose; nothing becomes real until it passes the gate and someone accountable says yes. Generation is not commitment.
            
            
            “Author the meaning once. Let the system handle the app.”
            
            
            AI here is an emissary — capable, attributed, bounded, and never sovereign.
            
          
            
          
            
            
            Claimipedia · the library · opening soon
            
            
            Disagreement you can read.
            
            
            Claims judged under stated, versioned review methods — with the reasoning shown. The same evidence can be Accepted under one method, Conflicted under another and Undecided under a third, and you can see exactly why.
            
            
            “The platform does not produce a universal truth score.”
            
            
            AI contributions arrive as proposals. Only a human reviewer can accept them.
            
          
            
          
            
            
            For the people who do the work
            
            
            Not a machine that thinks for you.
            
            
            A discipline that lets scientists, stewards, reviewers and whole communities work alongside AI — and still answer for what happens. We suspect it grows new crafts, too: covenant architects, evidence stewards, wardens of emissaries.
            
            
            “AI may propose; Temple decides admissibility.”
            
          
            
        
            
      
            

      
      
            
        
            XI · The ledger
            
        
            Where it stands, honestly.
            
        
            
          
              
            
            • Temple3 is a v0.1 laboratory. It is not a production system, and it has not been independently audited.
              • 
            
            • 101 of 101 checks passed in its latest integrated qualification run.
              • 
            
            • Three implementations — a JavaScript model, a Python verifier and a Rust library — are cross-checked on shared vectors. They share design lineage, so correlated mistakes remain possible.
              • 
            
            • 486 hostile cases in a differential corpus surfaced 26 semantic mismatches between them. After repair: zero.
              • 
            
            • Two Temples have federated in the lab. Networking, key rotation, encryption and zero-knowledge proofs are not built.
              • 
            
            • Today, Sourcery and Claimipedia run on Temple2 — the fact runtime Temple3 learns from.
              • 
            
            • The Ark is not yet sealed.
