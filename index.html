A real example

A biotech R&D team is deciding whether to trust a temperature-growth hypothesis.

One paper suggests heat stress reduces sustained growth above 34C. The team runs a simulation, ingests a lab measurement, sees a disagreement, and has to decide whether the hypothesis can still be promoted.

  1. Source claim

    Paper sets the expectation

    The claim enters the world with its source: heat stress appears to reduce sustained growth above 34C.

  2. Simulation witness

    The model predicts a path

    The team runs a witnessed simulation of the temperature-growth scenario and records a predicted final OD600 of 0.79.

  3. Observed measurement

    The lab reports what actually happened

    A Week 1 flask measurement arrives with provenance note and context. The observed final OD600 comes in at 0.72.

  4. Disagreement

    The gap becomes explicit

    Worldwright opens a disagreement record instead of hiding the mismatch in commentary or email.

  5. Governed decision

    The team can repair before it promotes

    Repair, policy, authority, and acceptance stay attached, so the final decision can be defended later by anyone who inherits it.

Why this helps

In Worldwright, the decision carries the paper, the simulation run, the measurement, the disagreement, the repair path, and the final accepted state.

The problem

R&D teams rarely lack evidence. They lack a shared world for it.

Fragmented by default

Papers, notebooks, models, spreadsheets, dashboards, and status updates all point at the same question, but usually live in different systems.

Trust breaks under pressure

The closer a team gets to a real decision, the more expensive it becomes to rely on memory, interpretation, or the loudest person in the room.

Context gets lost

A number without provenance, a model without witness, or a decision without history cannot travel very far before confidence degrades.

What Worldwright does

It keeps the whole decision path visible.

Worldwright is not another passive dashboard. It is a witnessed workspace where scientific evidence, simulation, measurement, disagreement, repair, policy, and decisions all stay in one inspectable model.

How it works

From evidence to decision, without losing context.

  1. Gather the live evidence

    Bring claims, source material, models, and observations into one shared workspace.

  2. Compare model and reality

    Run simulations, ingest measurements, and inspect the delta in the open.

  3. Govern the disagreement

    Make repair, policy, authority, and acceptance conditions visible before action.

  4. Advance with a witness

    Link the decision back to evidence and retain a replayable trail for what changed.

For real teams

Scientists, data scientists, engineers, managers, and founders can work from the same decision surface as coordination gets denser, without flattening their different roles.

Why it is different

Worldwright treats trust as part of the product surface.

Not a dashboard

Dashboards summarize outcomes. Worldwright supports the active process of comparing, repairing, accepting, and branching.

Not a black box

The path from evidence to decision stays visible. Teams can inspect why a conclusion changed and replay how they got there.

Built to scale with the team

It is designed for growing R&D teams, including medium-sized groups, where more people, more evidence, and more downstream consequence require stronger shared trust instead of heavier fragmentation.
